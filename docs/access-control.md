## Minimal roles

| Actor | Allowed product responsibility | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform administrator | Platform configuration, tenant provisioning, manual entitlements and confirmed future operating actions | Platform authority alone grants no tenant-private content/lead access, publication authority or blanket impersonation |
| Active tenant administrator | Own tenant's website/content/media, save/preview/publish/unpublish/restore; all tenant leads, assignment, status and authorized retention extensions; safe tenant member grants; explicit activation of its own verified active custom domain with valid TLS and eligible equivalent published pages | Backend must verify active membership and tenant/resource/action; no access or grant across tenants |
| Anonymous visitor | Eligible published website/derivatives and validated consultation submission | No drafts, private originals, lead lists, private lead receipt lookup or membership authority |

Clinic owner, manager, marketer, editor, professional and receptionist are target-user descriptions. They do not impose separate Owner/Manager/Editor/ClinicalReviewer/Publisher/Receptionist permission roles. A tenant administrator can both write and publish. Additional roles require a future confirmed need; no clinical reviewer role/gate is required by the current product.

“Initial assigned tenant administrator” means the first person assigned this same tenant-admin role for an organization, not another role, owner hierarchy or special lead permission. Platform administrators manage Godent; tenant administrators manage their own organization website/leads. Approved D01–D10 specify invitation-only provisioning, global email/password identity, verified email, atomic last-admin protection and controlled recovery. A local synthetic bootstrap flag is not a normal HTTP platform-grant path.

## Tenant and member grants

- A global identity does not confer tenant membership. Resolve and validate the active actor and tenant before resource reads/writes, lists, counts, search, export, suggestions, caches or privileged effects.
- An active tenant administrator may invite/grant/revoke the tenant-administrator membership only inside the current tenant, through backend-authorized operations with audit. Bind invitations to the intended identity and tenant, validate their lifecycle, and prevent replay or scope substitution. Invitations fix email/tenant/role, expire after 24 hours, are single use and are invalidated by resend/revoke. Concurrent membership changes and global disable must not orphan any tenant. No additional mandatory owner role is introduced.
- Tenant grants cannot create platform administrators, modify another tenant's memberships or expand platform authority. Membership revocation/inactivity must deny subsequent authorized requests and prevent assignment to that inactive member. Authentication/session handling must not treat stale client claims as current permission.
- A global user with memberships in more than one tenant must select a validated membership context. Changing a client tenant ID, host, resource ID, job payload or membership claim never substitutes for backend authorization.
- Platform provisioning/bootstrap is separate from normal tenant grants. Support access to private tenant data is not implied by platform operations. No time-limited support approval/impersonation workflow is selected in this baseline; any future mechanism needs a specific human decision and bounded authority.

## Ordinary location content and shared leads

A location is a tenant-owned content row, not a security principal, child tenant or administrative unit. Tenant consistency applies to location references exactly as to other content references. There are no location-scoped memberships, branch grants, branch lifecycle or branch queues.

Every active tenant administrator can see and manage all leads of that tenant. Location is optional inquiry information. Assignment identifies the responsible active administrator within the same tenant; it never hides the lead from other tenant administrators. Lists/search/counts/exports remain tenant-scoped. Reassignment, status/reopen and retention extensions retain actor/time/reason history under [workflows](workflows.md).

## Custom-domain activation

Only an active tenant administrator acting within the same tenant may explicitly activate its own verified active binding as primary, after valid TLS and equivalent eligible published pages are ready. Backend checks must reject inactive/revoked membership, another tenant’s binding and forged Host/query destinations, including races with binding/publication changes. Platform operations alone do not grant this tenant action. Activation is routing configuration, not content publication, a patient-content review gate or a new role. Follow the [primary URL contract](architecture.md#primary-url-and-page-redirects); temporary outages never implicitly activate/reverse a primary.

## Data and public boundaries

Use tenant keys, tenant-consistent foreign keys/uniqueness, backend checks and PostgreSQL row-level security. Runtime roles must not bypass protected tables; use transaction-bound tenant context and prove pooled/concurrent isolation. Public-read and worker contracts must not acquire broader access than their intended effect.

Private preview requires current tenant-administrator authorization and stays out of public caches and indexing. Current publication/media eligibility controls public delivery even for an immutable historical snapshot. Originals require authorized private access; signed URLs alone are not permission to publish. These boundaries also apply to jobs, domain resolution, media routes and SEO outputs.

Audit grants and important private-data/publication actions with redacted evidence. D4 audit retention is defined once in workflows. Product controls are not a legal compliance assertion.
